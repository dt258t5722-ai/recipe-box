Spaetzle

Little German egg dumplings, somewhere between a noodle and a dumpling, with a whisper of nutmeg. Just flour, eggs, milk, and salt, pressed straight into boiling water. Toss with butter, or crisp them in a hot pan the way it's done in Germany.

  • Tastes Better From Scratch
  • Serves 6
  • Dough rests 10 minutes
  • 2–3 minutes per batch, in boiling water

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1½ teaspoons salt
  • ¼ teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 4 large eggs, at room temperature
  • ½ cup milk

Method

Make the dough

  1. 01

    In a medium to large bowl, stir together the flour, salt, and nutmeg.

  2. 02

    In a second bowl, beat the eggs in one at a time until well blended, then whisk in the milk.

  3. 03

    Make a well in the center of the flour and pour in the egg mixture. Stir until a soft dough forms: thicker than a batter, spoonable but not pourable.

  4. 04

    Cover the bowl with a tea towel and let the dough rest for about 10 minutes.

Cook

  1. 05

    Bring 2 to 3 quarts of lightly salted water to a boil.

  2. 06

    Spoon about half the dough into a spaetzle maker, or onto a grater, colander, or steamer basket held over the pot, and push it through the holes with a spatula, letting it drop into the water.

  3. 07

    Stir gently. Cook 2 to 3 minutes, until the dumplings float to the top.

  4. 08

    Lift them out with a slotted spoon, drain in a colander, and turn into a bowl. Repeat with the rest of the dough.

  5. 09

    Toss with butter and serve warm, or brown them in melted butter in a large hot frying pan, turning once, until crisp on both sides.

Notes from the recipe

No spaetzle maker?. Press the dough through the large holes of a box grater, a colander, or a steamer basket with a spatula.

The dough. Aim for just thicker than batter: it should hold on a spoon but not pour.

To serve. Buttered, or pan-fried until crisp, with a sprinkle of chopped parsley.

Bacon and onion. Fry finely diced bacon and onion for a couple of minutes before adding the spaetzle to the pan.

Käsespätzle. Layer the warm spaetzle in a baking dish with shredded Swiss cheese between the layers, top with bacon and sautéed onions, and bake at 375°F for about 12 minutes.

Keeps. Several days, covered, in the fridge. Rewarm briefly in boiling water, or in the microwave with a splash of water and a little butter. Freezes for 3 to 4 months; thaw overnight in the fridge.
